Scene cards

2 min per card

One card per scene, before you write it. Enter at the latest possible moment.

Do: fill the card. Fill "Enter at" LAST — it forces the latest possible entry, which automatically tightens every scene.

Examples (each story's climactic scene):

"Backup": Server Room, Night · Nadia wants to press Y · Blocked by: his unfinished folder on screen · End: company saved (+), goodbye made real (−) · Enter at: her finger already over the key. "The Survey": Call Center, Late Night · Marcus wants to end the call · Blocked by: she asked him the one question nobody has in years · End: he answers honestly · Enter at: the pause after her question, supervisor's footsteps approaching. "The Return": Childhood Bedroom, Afternoon · Eli wants to find something and leave before the hour ends · Blocked by: Grace's handwriting inside a closet door — a calendar counted from Day 1 to today · End: he knows she waited; can't unknow it · Enter at: his hand already on the closet door.

The scene engine

Element Question
Goal What does the POV character want in this scene?
Conflict What blocks it?
Outcome Who wins (+/−)?
Turn What is different at the end vs. the start?

Enter late / exit early: on every opening ask can I start three lines later? On every ending: can I end three lines earlier? If yes, do it.

Scene patterns — twenty cards and five drills

Use when: you want to practice, or a scene needs a proven engine. Plug in your characters — the structure is done.

# Pattern The scene Enter at
01 The Delivery Someone brings news; the recipient already knows; neither acknowledges it Door already open, news implied by posture
02 The Request A needs something B will give only under conditions A can't meet Mid-negotiation, first offer already failed
03 The Interruption A third person forces a second conversation on top of a private one The private conversation at its most vulnerable
04 The Discovery Finding what they shouldn't — and deciding whether to pretend they didn't The moment of finding, not the approach
05 The Apology That Isn't Technically correct, emotionally absent The end of the apology — the other face
06 The Test One evaluates the other; both know; neither says so The test already running
07 The Clock A simple task before a deadline; one thing keeps going wrong The first thing going wrong, never the setup
08 The Return Back after a long absence; the place or person has changed The first moment the change is visible to them
09 The Reveal Information recontextualizes everything; no immediate reaction Just before it arrives; leave before the reaction completes
10 The Standoff Incompatible wants; neither moves first; an object between them The silence after the last move failed
11 The Performance Presenting / lying / celebrating / grieving in a way they don't feel Performance already underway
12 The Witness Watching what they shouldn't see; can't intervene, can't look away The thing already happening
13 The Handoff Responsibility passes; only one understands its full weight The handoff itself
14 The Pretense Two pretend a conversation never happened; a third almost notices Pretense in full operation
15 The Last Chance A door is closing; one thing left to say, almost unsaid The door already in motion; leave before it closes or stays open
16 The Replacement Doing what someone else used to do — and not quite managing The attempt already underway and already failing
17 The Agreement Both agree; only one means it; the scene is the gap The agreement being sealed
18 The Wait Something small keeps almost happening and then not happening Mid-wait, after the first near-miss already failed
19 The Wrong Room Somewhere they don't belong, trying to look like they do; ends at the moment of decision Already inside
20 The Goodbye Only one knows it's for good Mid-goodbye, the ritual already in progress

Five constraint drills:

Drill Constraint Trains
A · The 1-Page Scene Write any pattern in exactly one page — no more, no less Every line earning its place
B · Dialogue-Only Zero action lines except the slug; dialogue + parentheticals only Subtext forced into the lines
C · Action-Only Zero dialogue; entire conflict in behavior and objects Visual storytelling
D · Enter Impossibly Late Start 2/3 through the conflict; the reader infers what preceded Late entry as a reflex
E · The Reversal Take a scene you wrote; swap who has power at start vs. end so the reversal runs the opposite direction Structural flexibility
