What a screenplay is. The written blueprint a film or TV episode is made from — what the audience will see and hear, scene by scene, in a strict standard layout. It is not prose and not a finished movie; it's the plan everyone (director, actors, crew) works from. One correctly formatted page ≈ one minute on screen, which is why the layout is strict: page count is running time.

What this is. A screenplay page that formats itself. You type and press two or three keys; it puts every line in the right place. You never set a margin or a font, and there is nothing to install — the page, the format doctor, the page count and the exports are all in this one file.

What the professionals use. Final Draft is the industry standard, and its keys are the keys you are learning here: Enter, Tab, Ctrl+1…6 behave the same in both. When you want to move, Script ▾ → .fdx hands Final Draft your script with the formatting intact. Other clean routes: WriterDuet’s free tier, Trelby, StudioBinder. Routes that look free and are not: Arc Studio’s free tier cannot export a clean PDF, Fade In’s demo watermarks every page, Celtx’s free tier stamps a footer on your script.

How to read the keys. Ctrl+1 means hold Ctrl and tap 1. → means "then." Enter and Tab are the ordinary keys. That's the entire notation.

The twelve words you'll meet before they're taught (each gets a real definition in The page in six shapes or one plain line in the glossary — this is just so nothing stops you tonight):

Word Plain English
Scene One continuous piece of action in one place at one time
Slug (scene heading) The ALL-CAPS line that starts every scene: inside or outside + place + day or night
INT. / EXT. Interior (inside) / Exterior (outside) — the first word of every slug
Action line Present-tense description of what the camera sees
Dialogue The words characters speak
Draft One complete version of the script; Draft 1 just has to exist
Short / feature / pilot A short film script (this month: 3 scenes) / a full movie (90–120 pages) / a TV series' first episode
Logline The whole story in one sentence (the guide builds yours with a formula)
Beat The smallest unit of story — one moment where something changes
Protagonist The main character — the person the story happens to and through
.fdx Final Draft's file type, the way .docx is Word's
ScriptNote the page's sticky note pinned inside the script — your journal lives in one
